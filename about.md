About

Education in the U.S. has a history of inequality, dating back to the 1600s. This inequality has been perpetuated through factors such as socioeconomic status, segregation, and racism. While this issue has been attempted to be addressed through legislation, many scholars still perceive the U.S. education system as “separate and unequal” (Howell et al., 2). This website takes a deeper look into this inequality. Specifically, our team explores how the gap in performance between Black and white elementary school students is largely predicted by parental education. This gap has been proven to have a significant effect size in education literature, so we are further delving into this predictor and its implications. Throughout our project, we explore the intersectionality of education, generational patterns, and socioeconomic status. 

Sourcing
We sourced our data from the Stanford Educational Opportunity Project, which displays the academic performance of children in the U.S. grades 3-8. The data spans across 2009-2025. Students are grouped and divided according to their race, gender, the state in which they reside, and their families economic status. Furthermore, their scores on national assessments in subjects such as Math and RLA are also revealed. The researchers used data from the Department of Education’s National Center for Education Statistics (NCES), which receives data from the EDFacts Database. The researchers used the restricted files from this database to approximate test score distributions in districts, counties, administrative school districts, and commuting zones. In the data, schools are identified by NCES IDs that are linked to more data from The Common Core of Data (CCD). Using the CCD guidelines, researchers create the SEDA school ID. To create stable geographic identifiers, researchers used the school’s, commuting zone’s, administrative district’s, and county’s most recent geographical information. Additionally, researchers used heteroskedastic ordered probit modeling to approximate the proficiency thresholds within each state, grade, year, and subject. The researchers then estimate the model using the counts of all students in each geographic school district within the state, grade, year, and subject. 

Processing 
The original source of our data was already clean, although there were a few blank cells. In addition to the original data, we added a column that displayed each state’s political affiliation in the 2024 election. We decided to add this column to further our exploration into the correlation between geographical location and test scores by adding the variable of politics. 


Presenting 
This website has been created using GitHub Pages. This decision was made within our team because we found it the easiest to navigate and customize. Our team chose a polished, light theme to make our information and visualizations clearer and more accessible. For most of our visualizations, we used Tableau. This tool allowed us to create many visualization types, ranging from charts to maps. We were able to customize these visualizations through color and form, and we were able to choose accessible, user friendly options. In addition to the Tableau visualizations, our team used a collection of images that further emphasized the information presented.
